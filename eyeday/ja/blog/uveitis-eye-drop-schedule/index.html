How to keep a uveitis eye drop schedule clear

Jul 29, 2026

Uveitis can involve frequent eye drops and schedule changes. A clear record helps you follow the prescription you were given, not the version you half remember.

Uveitis needs medical follow-up. Do not start, stop or taper steroid drops such as Pred Forte without your ophthalmologist’s instructions.

Start with the exact plan from your clinic

Uveitis eye drop schedules can change during follow-up. Some people are told to use steroid drops such as Pred Forte often at first, then reduce them step by step. The important part is not to guess the taper from memory.

Write down the drug name, the eye, the daily count, and the date when any reduction starts. If your doctor is also watching eye pressure or glaucoma risk, keep those notes next to the dosing record.

Separate the things that are easy to confuse

  • Drug name, including brand names such as Pred Forte
  • Which eye each drop goes in
  • Start date, end date, and any taper date

If the plan changes after a visit, update the reminder the same day. That keeps yesterday’s schedule from silently becoming today’s mistake.

Using EyeDay for uveitis drops

EyeDay lets you keep each drop as its own schedule and log every completed dose. It is a reminder and record tool, not a treatment guide, so the prescription from your ophthalmologist should always come first.

If your uveitis drop schedule keeps changing

Keep each drop, eye, time and taper date in one place.
