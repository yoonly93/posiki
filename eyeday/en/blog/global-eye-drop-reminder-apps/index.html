Eye drop reminder apps and medication apps compared

Jul 27, 2026

Search for an eye drop reminder and you get eye-drop-specific apps, general medication reminders and your phone's built-in health app all at once. They are good at different things, so what to pick depends on whether your prescription is simple or complicated.

This is a general comparison based on public app listings and store descriptions. Features and available countries change over time.

EyeDropAlarm

EyeDropAlarm is an iPhone app focused on eye drop reminders. It leans on eye-drop-specific details: bottle colour, which eye a drop goes in, and the gap between drops.

Drop Reminder

Drop Reminder is an iPhone app presented for managing several drop schedules at once — after cataract surgery, for glaucoma, for dry eye. Its selling point is a simple flow with no account.

Medisafe

Medisafe is a widely known general medication reminder. It is strong on pills, supplements, caregiver alerts and adherence reports, but the finer eye-drop details — left versus right eye, a five-minute gap — may be something you have to arrange yourself.

MyTherapy

MyTherapy also combines medication reminders with health records. It is good for tracking medication, measurements and appointments broadly, but its screens are not laid out around eye drops the way a dedicated app like EyeDay is.

Medications in Apple Health

On iPhone you can set up basic medication reminders in the Health app. The advantage is that you install nothing extra; the limits show up around eye-drop interval warnings and a flow built on dosing records.

EyeDay

EyeDay was built for eye drops from the start. You register the drug name, left eye, right eye or both, the daily count, the start time and a custom schedule, each as its own field.

When you use several drops in a row, it checks the gap against your recent doses. People on long-term drops for glaucoma can keep eye pressure records alongside, and for a fixed course after cataract or laser surgery you can see today's doses on a timeline.

It also supports Korean, Japanese, English and other languages, and runs on both iPhone and Android. It is aimed at people for whom 'did the drop actually go in' matters more than a simple pill reminder.

So which should you choose?

  • If you want pills and supplements handled together, a general medication reminder may be easier.
  • If you have one drop and only need a nudge, your phone's built-in reminder may be enough.
  • If you have several drops, or you need left/right eye, five-minute gaps and eye pressure records, a dedicated eye drop app fits better.
