Steroid eye drops

What to watch when using steroid eye drops after cataract surgery

Jul 27, 2026

Steroid drops are often prescribed after cataract surgery. What matters is less the name of the drug than following the count and duration your clinic gave you.

Steroid eye drops sometimes need monitoring, including of eye pressure. Do not start or stop them on your own; follow your ophthalmologist's instructions.

Don't stop just because it feels better

A few days after surgery, once your eye is comfortable, it is tempting to cut back or stop. But a post-op prescription is not set by symptoms alone. Controlling inflammation, preventing infection and monitoring recovery are all reasons you are asked to keep going for a set period.

The reverse is also unwise: using leftover steroid drops on your own because something feels off. Whether the medication needs adjusting, or something else is going on, is what an appointment is for.

Mark the days the count steps down

Post-op drops often start frequent and then step down every few days or weeks. Miss one of those change dates and you may use too much, or taper too early.

  • Photograph the prescription handout
  • Mark the step-down dates in a calendar or an app
  • Don't share one bottle with family

Managing the course in EyeDay

When you add a drug in EyeDay, entering the daily count and start time separately makes the repeating post-op schedule easy to follow. On the day the count changes, edit the existing schedule or register a new one to keep it unambiguous.
