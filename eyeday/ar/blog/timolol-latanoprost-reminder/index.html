Glaucoma drug names

How not to forget timolol and latanoprost drops

Jul 27, 2026

Glaucoma drops come with a generic name, a brand name and a bottle shape that all differ, which makes them feel complicated from the patient's side. What matters is using the drug you were prescribed, the way you were told, consistently.

This is not a recommendation to use any particular drug. Whether timolol, latanoprost or anything else suits you is a decision for your doctor.

A change of name makes it more confusing

During a visit you may be switched to a different product in the same class, or the brand name may look different depending on the clinic and the pharmacy. That is when patients start wondering 'is this the same as the old one?' or 'do I take both?'. Always check with the clinic or pharmacy, and never use two of them together on your own.

Register the product name in the app exactly as written, and put the generic name or your clinic's explanation in a note so it is easy to check later.

Once-a-day drops are easy to miss too

A once-daily drop looks simple, but missing it means losing the whole day. The bedtime one is easy to forget when you are tired; the morning one slips while you are getting ready. So rather than 'it's only once a day, it's fine', the useful thought is 'it's only once a day, so I'll make sure to check it off'.

The record helps at your appointment too

When eye pressure is not coming down as hoped, it can matter whether the issue is the drug itself or the timing having drifted. The judgement is your care team's, but walking in with a dosing record makes the conversation far more specific.

If you keep forgetting your glaucoma drops

Register the drug under its own name and tick it off every day.
